## Overview

This policy explains how **{{site.controllerName}}** meets its obligations under the UK General Data Protection Regulation (UK GDPR) and the Data Protection Act 2018, and the standards I hold myself to when handling personal data.

The [Privacy notice](/privacy/) tells you what personal data I collect, why, and what your rights are. This policy describes how I look after that data in practice. It applies to personal data I handle about website visitors, clients, learners and their parents or carers, event attendees, contributors, mailing-list subscribers and anyone else who contacts me.

**{{site.controllerName}}** is the controller for the personal data described in the [Privacy notice](/privacy/). For personal data that a client asks me to handle for their own purposes, I may instead act as a processor and will say so in our agreement.

## Principles

I handle personal data in line with the seven UK GDPR principles:

- **Lawfulness, fairness and transparency:** I have a lawful basis for each use of personal data, I use it in ways people would reasonably expect, and I explain it in the privacy notice
- **Purpose limitation:** I collect data for specified, legitimate purposes and do not use it in ways that are incompatible with them
- **Data minimisation:** I collect only what I need
- **Accuracy:** I keep data accurate and correct it when I learn it is wrong
- **Storage limitation:** I keep data no longer than necessary, as set out in the [Data retention policy](/retention/)
- **Integrity and confidentiality:** I protect data with appropriate security, as set out in the [Information security policy](/informationsecurity/)
- **Accountability:** I can show how I comply, and I keep records

## Lawful bases

For each purpose, I rely on one of the lawful bases in Article 6 of the UK GDPR, for example:

- **Contract:** to take bookings, deliver services and handle payments
- **Legal obligation:** to keep accounting and tax records, and to meet safeguarding duties
- **Legitimate interests:** to run, secure and improve my services, and to respond to enquiries, after weighing these interests against people’s rights
- **Consent:** for optional uses such as marketing emails, non-essential cookies and, where relied on, photography and recordings. Consent can be withdrawn at any time
- **Vital interests or public task:** only in the rare cases where they genuinely apply, for example an emergency

The lawful basis for each purpose is recorded in the privacy notice and in my internal records of processing.

## Special category and children’s data

Special category data, such as information about health, disability, ethnicity or religion, needs extra care. I collect it only where it is necessary, for example to make reasonable adjustments for a learner or attendee, and I rely on an appropriate Article 9 condition. I will not ask for it if I do not need it.

Children’s data gets particular protection. For tutoring and safeguarding I work with parents or carers, collect the minimum, store records securely, and follow the [Safeguarding policy](/safeguarding/) and the [Tutoring terms](/tutoring/). I do not target children with marketing, and I do not profile them.

Safeguarding information may need to be shared without consent where this is necessary to protect a child or adult at risk. This is explained in the [Safeguarding policy](/safeguarding/).

## Your rights

You have rights over your personal data, including the right to be informed, to access it, to have it corrected or erased, to restrict or object to its use, to data portability where it applies, and to object to direct marketing. You also have rights about automated decisions. I do not make decisions about individuals based solely on automated processing that have legal or similarly significant effects.

To use a right, contact {{site.privacyEmail | mailto}}. In general:

- I will respond within one month. If a request is complex or I have received several, I may extend this by up to two further months and I will tell you why
- I will not charge a fee unless a request is manifestly unfounded or excessive
- I may ask for proof of identity, and for details that help me find the information
- Some rights depend on the lawful basis, and some information may be withheld where the law allows or requires, for example to protect another person’s data or a safeguarding investigation. If I do this, I will explain

## Using other organisations

I use other organisations to help provide services, such as hosting, email, payments, booking, analytics and video-conferencing providers. Where a provider handles personal data for me, I:

- Choose providers with appropriate security and privacy commitments
- Make sure there is a contract or terms that meet UK GDPR requirements
- Check where data is processed and whether transfers outside the UK have appropriate safeguards, such as an adequacy decision, the UK International Data Transfer Agreement or the UK Addendum to the EU Standard Contractual Clauses
- Only share what the provider needs

## Privacy by design and risk assessments

Before I start a new use of personal data, such as a new tool, form, integration or service, I consider the privacy risk. Where a use is likely to result in a high risk to people’s rights, for example large-scale handling of children’s data, I carry out a data protection impact assessment first.

When I build tools and websites, I aim to collect no personal data unless it is needed, and to keep processing local to the person’s browser where this is practical.

## Personal data breaches

A personal data breach is a security incident that leads to accidental or unlawful loss, alteration, disclosure of or access to personal data.

If a breach happens, I will:

1. Act quickly to contain it and reduce harm
2. Assess the risk to the people affected
3. Report it to the Information Commissioner’s Office (ICO) within 72 hours of becoming aware of it, unless it is unlikely to result in a risk to people’s rights and freedoms
4. Tell the people affected without undue delay if there is a high risk to them
5. Record the breach, what I did and what I changed

The wider response process is in the [Information security policy](/informationsecurity/).

## Marketing and cookies

I send direct marketing only where the law allows, for example with your consent or in line with the “soft opt-in” rules for existing customers, and every message has a simple way to opt out. I follow the Privacy and Electronic Communications Regulations (PECR) for cookies and similar technologies. Cookie choices are explained in the [cookie policy](/cookies/).

## Records and accountability

I keep an up-to-date record of the personal data I process, the purposes, the lawful bases, recipients, retention periods and security measures. I review it at least once a year and whenever I change what I do.

I keep this policy, the privacy notice and the retention schedule consistent with each other and with what I actually do.
