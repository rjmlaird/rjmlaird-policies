## Our commitment

The welfare of children, young people and adults at risk comes first. I am committed to creating safe, respectful learning and working environments, to taking concerns seriously and to acting on them promptly.

This policy applies to **{{site.controllerName}}** and to anyone I engage to help deliver tutoring, mentoring, workshops, talks, events or online activities. It applies whether activities take place in person, online or in writing.

**If someone is in immediate danger, call 999.**

## Who this policy covers

- **Children and young people:** anyone under 18.
- **Adults at risk:** adults who may be unable to protect themselves from harm or exploitation because of their circumstances, for example because of disability, illness, or being coerced or controlled.

This policy applies to learners, workshop and event participants, and anyone else I come into contact with through my work who may be at risk of harm.

## Principles

- Everyone has the right to be safe and to be treated with respect.
- Concerns are taken seriously, recorded and acted on, whoever raises them.
- Safeguarding is everyone’s responsibility, and sharing a worry is not an accusation.
- Information is shared only with those who need to know, but concerns about a child’s safety are never kept secret.
- Children, young people and adults at risk are listened to, and their wishes and feelings are taken into account.

## Recognising concerns

Concerns can arise from something a person says or does, from changes in behaviour, from something seen or heard during a session, or from information passed on by someone else. Signs of possible abuse or neglect can include physical injuries without a clear explanation, sudden changes in mood or behaviour, fear of a particular person or place, being withdrawn, poor hygiene or inadequate care, or disclosing something troubling.

Abuse and neglect can be physical, emotional, sexual, or through neglect, and can also include bullying, online harm, exploitation, radicalisation and coercive control. These signs do not prove that abuse is happening, but they should never be ignored.

## Responding to a concern

If a child or adult tells me something worrying, or I am worried about them:

1. I will listen calmly, take what is said seriously and not promise to keep it secret.
2. I will not ask leading questions or investigate myself; I will only ask what is needed to understand what was said.
3. I will make a written record as soon as possible, using the person’s own words where I can, with the date, time and who was involved.
4. If there is immediate risk, I will call 999.
5. Otherwise I will contact the relevant local authority children’s services or adult social care, or the police, without delay, and I will tell the parent or carer unless doing so could put the person at greater risk.
6. I will follow up to check that action has been taken.

You can also contact the NSPCC helpline on 0808 800 5000 for advice about a child, and Childline on 0800 1111 is available for children and young people.

## Allegations about me or those working with me

If a concern is raised about my own conduct, or the conduct of anyone working with me, I will take it seriously, stop any activity that could put anyone at risk, and contact the local authority designated officer (LADO) for the relevant area for advice. I will cooperate fully with any investigation and will not try to deal with the matter alone.

## Safe working practice

- Sessions with children and young people are planned and agreed with a parent or carer.
- I do not arrange to meet or communicate privately with a child outside agreed channels, and I do not use personal social media accounts to contact learners.
- Online sessions take place on agreed platforms. I do not ask a child to share personal contact details, and I keep communications professional, relevant and recorded where appropriate.
- I will not give a child gifts or favours, and I will not be alone with a child in a way that puts them or me at risk. Where in-person work is needed, it takes place in an open, agreed and appropriate setting.
- Physical contact is avoided except where it is necessary for a child’s safety.
- I expect everyone taking part to behave respectfully. Bullying, harassment and discrimination are not tolerated.

More detail for tutoring is in the [tutoring terms](/tutoring/). Rules about images and recordings are in the [photography and media consent policy](/mediaconsent/).

## Online safety

Online sessions and online communication carry particular risks. I use reputable platforms with sensible privacy settings, keep sessions within agreed times, do not record sessions unless this has been agreed in line with the media consent policy, and report any online harm or inappropriate contact. Parents and carers are encouraged to know when and where an online session is taking place.

## Confidentiality and information sharing

Safeguarding information is treated confidentially and is shared only with people who need it to keep someone safe, such as children’s services, adult social care, the police or other relevant agencies. Data-protection law does not prevent information being shared where it is necessary to protect a child or adult at risk. See the [privacy notice](/privacy/) for how personal information is handled.
