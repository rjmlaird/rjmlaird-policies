## Overview

This policy explains how **{{site.controllerName}}** uses artificial-intelligence (AI) tools in research, writing, code, design, teaching and client work, and the limits I set on that use.

AI tools can speed up routine work and help me explore ideas. They also make mistakes, can reproduce bias and can put information at risk if they are used carelessly. I use them as assistants, not as authorities. This policy applies with the [Website disclaimer](/disclaimer/), the [Privacy notice](/privacy/) and the [Information security policy](/informationsecurity/).

## Where I use AI

Depending on the task, I may use AI tools to:

- Summarise, outline, translate or edit text
- Suggest, explain, test or review code
- Analyse or tidy data, or draft charts and tables
- Generate or adapt images, diagrams and layouts
- Search, organise or compare sources during research

I do not use AI for every task, and I do not use it where the risk outweighs the benefit.

## Principles

- **I stay accountable.** I review and take responsibility for everything I publish or deliver, whether or not an AI tool helped to produce it. “The AI wrote it” is not a defence.
- **I check facts and sources.** I verify claims, figures, quotations and references against reliable sources before I rely on them. AI tools can invent references, so I do not cite a source I have not checked.
- **I protect confidential information.** I do not paste personal data, client confidential material or commercially sensitive information into an AI tool unless I have a lawful basis and the authority to do so, and I have checked how that tool handles the data.
- **I am open about it.** I do not present AI-generated work as something it is not. Where AI has played a material part in a deliverable, I will say so if you ask, and I will follow any rules you set.
- **I watch for bias and harm.** I review output for stereotypes, exclusion, inaccessible content and misleading framing, and I correct or discard it.
- **I keep people in the loop.** I do not use AI to make decisions about individuals that have legal or similarly significant effects.

## What I will not do

I will not:

- Enter children’s or learners’ personal information into a general-purpose AI tool
- Enter special category data or safeguarding information into an AI tool
- Use client material to train or fine-tune a model without your written agreement
- Use AI to impersonate a real person, create deceptive images or recordings, or produce content that breaches the [Acceptable use policy](/acceptableuse/)
- Use AI to write or submit work that a learner is meant to produce for assessment

## Choosing and checking tools

Before I use a new AI tool for anything beyond low-risk tasks, I look at:

- Whether my inputs are used to train the provider’s models, and whether I can opt out
- How long inputs and outputs are kept, and who can see them
- Where data is processed, and whether appropriate transfer safeguards apply
- The provider’s security measures, terms and privacy information
- Whether a business or enterprise plan with stronger data protections is needed

I use accounts with strong passwords and multi-factor authentication, in line with the [Information security policy](/informationsecurity/).

## Client work

If I use AI on a client project:

- I will follow any client policy on AI that you tell me about
- I will not use your confidential information in an AI tool unless we have agreed this, or the tool is one you have approved
- Ownership of the final deliverables is as set out in our agreement. AI-generated material may attract weaker or different intellectual-property protection than human-created work, so please ask if this matters for what you are commissioning
- You should still review deliverables for accuracy and suitability before you use them

If you would like me not to use AI on your work, tell me when you book or in the proposal.

## Tutoring and learners

Learning support is about understanding, not only getting answers.

- I may show learners how AI tools work and where they fail, in a way that suits their age and the rules that apply to them
- Learners must follow the rules of their school, college, exam board or institution on AI use in assessed work. I will not help anyone present AI-generated work as their own where that is not allowed
- Where a learner uses an AI tool in a session, the parent or carer is responsible for the account and for checking the tool’s age requirements
- I do not enter learners’ details into AI tools, as set out above

See the [Tutoring terms](/tutoring/) and the [Safeguarding policy](/safeguarding/).

## Environmental impact

AI tools use energy and water. I try to use them where they save meaningful effort, to avoid pointless or repeated requests, and to prefer smaller or more efficient tools for simple tasks.
